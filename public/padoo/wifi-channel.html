Fix a stuttering Wi-Fi connection

If the cursor catches about twice a second on Wi-Fi but feels smooth on a cable, your router's channel may be the cause. It usually takes a few minutes to change.

Is this your problem?

It's a good match if all of these are true:

  • Movement catches in a steady rhythm, not at random.
  • Padoo is smooth when the iPhone is connected by cable.
  • Your Mac is on a 5 GHz channel other than 44 or 149. To check, hold Option and click the Wi-Fi icon in the Mac's menu bar, then look for Channel.

If your Mac is already on 44 or 149, this probably isn't the cause. Support can help from there.

Why it happens

Your iPhone's Wi-Fi radio does two jobs. It talks to your router, and it keeps a small private link with your other Apple devices for features like AirDrop. That private link normally uses channel 44 or channel 149.

When your router is on a different channel, the radio has to keep leaving your network to visit the private link's channel. Nothing gets through while it's away. You wouldn't notice that while streaming a video, but a cursor shows every gap straight away.

When your router uses the same channel, the radio doesn't have to leave.

Your router isn't faulty. On its Auto setting it picks a channel that looks quiet, and it has no way of knowing about Apple's link. It can also move to a new channel after a restart, so this can appear out of nowhere.

Which channel to choose

44Try this first. It's the channel we tested, and it's widely available.
149Try this if 44 doesn't help, especially in the US. It isn't offered in every country. We haven't tested it ourselves; it comes from public research on Apple's link.

How to change it

Every router's screens look different, so the names below may not match exactly.

  1. Open your router's settings. The address and login are usually on a label on the router, or in its app.
  2. Find the wireless settings for 5 GHz. If your router combines its bands under one name, often called Smart Connect, you may need to turn that off first to see the channel setting.
  3. Change the channel from Auto to 44. Leave the channel width on 80 MHz or Auto.
  4. Save. Your Wi-Fi will drop for a moment while the router restarts it.
  5. Try Padoo again. Check that your Mac and iPhone have reconnected on 5 GHz. If the stutter is still there, try 149 where it's available.

Before you change it: Apple generally recommends leaving routers on Auto, so this is a fix for this specific problem. A fixed channel won't move away from a busy neighbor. If the rest of your Wi-Fi gets worse, set it back to Auto.

Common questions

I can't change my router's channel.

Some provider routers and mesh systems don't allow it. Use a cable with Padoo, or ask your provider whether the channel can be set.

Will turning off AirDrop fix it?

It didn't in our testing. iOS can keep the private link running with AirDrop off.

Will this affect my other devices?

Most devices that use 5 GHz will simply follow the router to the new channel. Very old devices that only use 2.4 GHz aren't affected.

Does this apply to Windows?

We've only measured this with a Mac. The iPhone's radio works the same way, so it may help on Windows too.
