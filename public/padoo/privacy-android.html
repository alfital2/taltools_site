Privacy Policy: Padoo for Android

Last updated: November 2026

Padoo turns an Android phone into a trackpad, air pointer, keyboard and app deck for a computer. It is made of this Android app and a free companion app for Mac or Windows that you install on your own computer. Looking for the iPhone app? See the iPhone privacy policy.

In short

Padoo has no accounts, no sign-in and no ads, and it never tracks you across apps or websites. What you do with the trackpad, what you type or say, and what is on your computer stay between your own devices.

To find out where Padoo works and where it gets stuck, the app sends anonymous usage statistics. They are on unless you turn them off, and you can turn them off at any time.

Anonymous usage statistics

The app sends short, anonymous events to TelemetryDeck, an analytics service based in Germany that stores data in the EU. Examples: "the intro was finished", "the first computer was found", "pairing succeeded", "the unlock screen was shown", "Padoo crashed".

An event can include:

  • which step was reached (for example: pairing started, pairing succeeded),
  • how a connection was made or why it failed, as a category such as "timed out", never an error message,
  • how long connecting or a session took, how many times it reconnected, whether the computer was a Mac or a Windows PC, and which Padoo features were used (trackpad, air pointer, keyboard, mic typing, dock, deck, sliders, more than one computer),
  • crash and freeze counts with the app version only, never the crash details,
  • purchase steps (unlock screen shown, purchase started, completed or cancelled, purchases restored) and, for a completed purchase, the product, its price, currency and your country setting,
  • the app version, the phone model, the Android version, and your language and region setting.

An event never includes your name, your Google account, your email, your computer's name, the names of your apps, anything you type or say, your location or your contacts.

Events are tied to a random identifier that is created by the app, hashed on your phone, and hashed again with a salt on TelemetryDeck's servers before anything is stored, so it identifies an install, not a person. It is deleted when you uninstall Padoo. Nothing is shared with advertisers or data brokers, and nothing is combined with data from other apps. Events travel over HTTPS. Events not yet sent wait on the phone until they can be sent.

Turning it off: on the home screen ("Which computer?"), tap the "..." menu and turn off Share Anonymous Usage. From then on nothing is sent, not even a note that you turned it off, and events waiting to be sent are deleted.

Pairing code scanner (Google ML Kit)

Padoo reads the pairing QR code with Google's ML Kit barcode scanner, which runs entirely on your phone. The camera image is never stored or sent anywhere. ML Kit itself sends Google limited technical information to measure its performance and fix problems: device and app information (such as phone model, Android version, app name and version), performance metrics, the scanner's settings, event types and error codes, and a per-installation identifier that is not meant to identify you or your phone. Google says it uses this only for diagnostics and usage analytics, sends it over HTTPS and does not pass it to third parties. See Google's ML Kit data disclosure.

Purchases (Google Play)

Padoo is free to try for three days. The unlock and Multi-Computer Control are one-time purchases made through Google Play. Google handles payment and your payment details; we never see them. The app asks Google Play which Padoo products your Google account owns, and keeps a local note of that on your phone. Google's privacy policy applies to the payment.

Report a Problem

The same "..." menu has Report a Problem. It prepares an email to us in your own email app (or a share sheet if none is set up) with Padoo's log from your phone attached, plus recent crash reports. The report lists the app version, phone model and Android version, the connection status and the last message on screen, how many computers are visible and paired, your trial or purchase status, and whether usage sharing is on. The log lists computer names, the names of apps in front on your computer, and connection steps, never what you type. You see the whole email first, you can delete anything from it, and nothing is sent unless you send it. We use what you send only to answer you and fix the problem, and we delete it when it is no longer needed for that.

What travels between your devices

Apart from the statistics and the ML Kit information above, Padoo's network traffic is between your phone and your computer, on your own local network or over Bluetooth. It is encrypted with a key that only your two devices hold. It carries:

  • Your input: finger positions and movement, pointer motion from the phone's motion sensors while the air pointer is on, button and key presses, and the text you type or dictate, so your computer can act on them.
  • Your phone's make and model and its screen size, so your computer can list it and scale the pointer.
  • The icons and names of the apps in your computer's Dock or taskbar, sent to the phone so the app deck can show them.
  • The name of the app in front on your computer, so the phone can offer the right buttons for it. Nothing about what you do inside that app is read or sent.
  • Your computer's volume and brightness levels, so the sliders can show them.
  • Timing information used to measure and correct latency.

The receiving device uses this in real time and does not keep it. None of it is sent to us or anywhere outside your paired devices.

What stays on your phone

  • Pairing keys are created during pairing and stored in the app's private storage on your phone, encrypted with a key held in the Android Keystore. They never leave it.
  • Your settings (rotation lock, screen arrangement, mic language, which tips you have seen) stay on the phone.
  • A diagnostic log of what Padoo does, kept small and in the app's private storage, to help troubleshoot a connection. It leaves the phone only if you send it with Report a Problem.
  • Crash reports are written to the app's private storage for the same purpose.

Padoo does not back up its data to your Google account. Uninstalling Padoo deletes everything it stored.

Permissions, and why

  • Camera: only to scan the pairing code your computer shows. No photo is taken, stored or sent. You can type the code instead.
  • Nearby devices (Bluetooth): only when your computer turns on DuoLink, to send pointer motion straight to your own computer over Bluetooth, encrypted. Padoo asks the first time it is needed.
  • Microphone: only while you use mic typing. Padoo passes your speech to Android's speech recognition service and receives text back; Padoo itself never records, stores or sends audio. On Android 12 and later, where the phone has on-device recognition, Padoo uses it so speech stays on the phone. Otherwise the phone's default speech service is used (usually Google's), which may process the audio online under its own privacy policy. Only the resulting text goes to your computer.
  • Network access and Wi-Fi state: to find your computer on your network and connect to it, and to keep Wi-Fi responsive while you use the trackpad.
  • Foreground service: to keep the connection for a short time after you switch away from Padoo, so coming back is instant. A notification shows while it runs.
  • Screenshot detection (Android 14 and later): Padoo is told when a screenshot is taken while it is open, only to recognise a phone's three-finger screenshot gesture and suggest turning it off. Padoo never sees the screenshot.
  • Motion sensors (no permission needed): while the air pointer is on, to work out where you are aiming, and while the deck is visible, to move the light across its keys.

Your rights

Laws such as the GDPR and the CCPA give you rights to access, correct, export or delete personal data a company holds about you. The usage statistics are anonymous, so we cannot pick out yours, but you can stop them at any time with the switch above. If you have emailed us, ask and we will delete your messages.

Children

Padoo is a general audience utility and is not directed at children. The anonymous statistics are the same for everyone and identify no one.
