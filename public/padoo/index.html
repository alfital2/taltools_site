Three surfaces, one phone.

One piece of glass that switches behaviour, picked from a radial menu rather than a tab bar, because the surface's whole job is to be empty.

Trackpad

The glass behaves like the trackpad you already know, and every threshold behind it is tunable from the Mac, because your hands are not the average of everyone's hands.

  • Tap to click, two-finger tap to right click
  • Two fingers to scroll
  • Tap-and-a-half to click and drag
  • Three-finger swipe between desktops

Air pointer

Point the phone at the screen and the cursor follows, like a remote. A different mechanism, not the trackpad with another sensor: it sends an absolute position, so there is no acceleration curve between you and the screen.

  • Relative mode works instantly
  • Nothing to calibrate - it tracks rotation, not position
  • A halo on the Mac shows what is being tracked

Streamer deck

A grid of glass keys for the apps you choose, with the real icons. Press a key and that app comes forward. It's your layout - set it up once on the Mac, not a blind mirror of your Dock.

  • Choose and reorder the apps yourself
  • Brightness and volume as live sliders
  • Keys are modelled as glass, not pictures of buttons

How it behaves.

Three things that show up the moment you use it.

About two milliseconds

Two milliseconds from finger to cursor. The phone sends at 120 Hz, and always picks the fastest path itself - cable, direct link, or your router.

Feel

Sound and haptic fire from one pattern, on one timeline, so a click lands as a single event instead of an echo. A strip key gets its own two-note rise, so your hand can tell a button from a click without looking.

Your shortcuts

Padoo asks your Mac what a shortcut is actually bound to, then presses that - not Apple's default. Turn it off, and it presses nothing, instead of typing into whatever you had open.

Nothing leaves your two devices.

No account. No sign-in. No analytics. No servers, so there is no backend to send anything to. Your phone talks to your Mac and to nothing else. Pairing keys live in the keychain, and every session is authenticated.
